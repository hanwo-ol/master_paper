## 1. Physical Justifications of Spatial Projections and Temporal Window Thresholds
The biomechanical and physiological justifications for the fixed temporal and spatial constants utilized in this gait analysis pipeline are detailed below.

### 1-1. Gait Axis Alignment Window ($t_{\text{start\_window}}$, $t_{\text{end\_window}}$)
To align the coordinate system with the subject's primary walking direction, we define the forward and lateral axes using median filter windows of **18 frames (300 ms at 60 Hz)** at the start and end of each trial. A 300 ms window captures the quiet standing posture prior to gait initiation and the decelerative trajectory post-6m walking, smoothing out high-frequency marker noise without loss of kinematic posture.

### 1-2. Gait Initiation Buffer ($P_{\text{start}}$)
The initial baseline pelvis coordinate is calculated as the median pelvis position over the first 5 frames of the trial. At a 60 Hz sampling rate, **5 frames correspond to 83.3 ms**. This is shorter than the physiological lower limit of human reaction time, which stands at **~100–200 ms (Luce, 1986)**. Using this buffer guarantees that the baseline position is established entirely during the quiet standing phase before any voluntary muscular activation or anticipatory movement occurs.

### 1-3. Digital Smoothing Filter for Foot Trajectories
Prior to peak detection, we smooth the relative forward foot position $D_{\text{heel}}(t)$ using a moving average filter with an **80 ms window** (5 frames). This 80 ms window filters out high-frequency marker jitter (>10 Hz) caused by tracking noise, while preserving the fundamental biomechanical trajectory of the foot (which operates at a lower frequency range of ~1–2 Hz).

### 1-4. Pelvis Displacement Threshold for Gait Onset ($P_{\text{onset}}$)
The onset of walking ($t_{\text{onset}}$) is defined as the frame when the forward pelvis position shifts by **2 cm (0.02 m)** from $P_{\text{start}}$. A 2 cm forward displacement is chosen to match the postural shift during the **Anticipatory Postural Adjustment (APA) phase**, where the body's center of mass moves forward prior to toe-off (Mancini et al., 2016).

## 2. Bilateral Kinematic Baseline Calibration
To eliminate unilateral bias in clearance calculations, we pool bilateral marker coordinates to define the floor baseline:

1. **Velocity Computation**: Instantaneous 3D velocity vectors are calculated for both heel and toe markers:
   $$\vec{v}_{\text{heel}}(t) = \frac{\Delta \vec{x}_{\text{heel}}(t)}{\Delta t}, \quad \vec{v}_{\text{toe}}(t) = \frac{\Delta \vec{x}_{\text{toe}}(t)}{\Delta t}$$
2. **Stance Identification**: Stance frames are defined where the marker velocity falls below $0.15\text{ m/s}$:
   $$\mathcal{T}_{\text{stance}} = \left\{ t \;\middle|\; \|\vec{v}_{\text{heel}}(t)\|_2 < 0.15 \text{ or } \|\vec{v}_{\text{toe}}(t)\|_2 < 0.15 \right\}$$
3. **Bilateral Floor Baseline**: The floor level ($y_{\text{floor}}$) is calculated as the 5th percentile of the vertical coordinates ($Y$) of all heel and toe markers of **both left and right feet** across the active stance frames:
   $$y_{\text{floor}} = P_{5} \left( \left\{ y_{\text{heel}, L}(t), y_{\text{toe}, L}(t), y_{\text{heel}, R}(t), y_{\text{toe}, R}(t) \right\} \;\middle|\; t \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{stance}} \right)$$

## 3. OLS Model Diagnostics and Assumptions Verification
For the representative trial cohort ($N=98$, Seed 42), we systematically tested ordinary least squares (OLS) model assumptions to verify that covariate adjustment did not inflate regression variance or yield spurious coefficients.

### 3-1. Collinearity Evaluation (VIF)
To check if collinearity between age and disease group inflated model standard errors, we calculated the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) for the model regressors:
$$\text{VIF}_j = \frac{1}{1 - R_j^2}$$
* **Results**:
  - `group_code` (PD vs. HC): **1.7276**
  - `age`: **1.7725**
  - `sex_code` (Male vs. Female): **1.1443**
* **Interpretation**: VIF values are well below the standard threshold of 5.0 (and the conservative threshold of 10.0), indicating that the OLS model can partition the independent effects of age and disease without variance inflation.

### 3-2. Normality of Residuals (Shapiro-Wilk)
To test if residuals were normally distributed, we applied the Shapiro-Wilk test:
* **Stride Time ($stride\_time\_s$)**: $W = 0.9759$, $p = 0.0686$. The hypothesis of normally distributed residuals is accepted at $\alpha = 0.05$.
* **Step Length ($step\_length\_m$)**: $W = 0.9722$, $p = 0.0356$. The slight deviation from normality ($p < 0.05$) supports reporting parallel non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test results.

### 3-3. Homoscedasticity (Breusch-Pagan)
Constant variance of residuals was verified using the Breusch-Pagan test:
* **Stride Time**: $\chi^2(3) = 7.7940$, $p = 0.0505$. Constant variance is maintained.
* **Step Length**: $\chi^2(3) = 4.1189$, $p = 0.2489$. Constant variance is maintained.

### 3-4. Influence Diagnostics (Cook's Distance)
To verify that the OLS regressions were not driven by individual leverage points or outliers, we computed Cook's Distance ($D_i$) for each subject:
$$D_i = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^n (\hat{y}_j - \hat{y}_{j(i)})^2}{p \cdot s^2}$$
* **Results**: Maximum Cook's distance was **0.2137** (Stride Time) and **0.1348** (Step Length). The absence of values exceeding 1.0 confirms that no high-leverage points distorted the OLS coefficients.

## 4. Loss Function Formulation for Cohort-Level Optimization
The unsupervised cohort loss function $L(\theta)$ optimized parameters $\theta = (d, r, m_0)$ ($d$: `min_distance`, $r$: `prominence_ratio`, $m_0$: `min_prominence_m`) to detect Heel Strike (HS) and Toe Off (TO) events:

$$L_m(\theta) = \text{Loss}_{\text{periodicity}, m}(\theta) + \text{Reg}_{\text{count}, m}(\theta) + \text{Reg}_{\text{bounds}, m}(\theta) + \text{Reg}_{\text{balance}, m}(\theta)$$

1. **Periodicity Loss**: Measures stride time variability ($CV$); penalizes trial failure ($N_{\text{stride}} < 3$) with a default penalty value of $2.0$.
2. **Count Constraint**: Penalizes stride counts outside the standard physiological range of $[3, 9]$ strides for a 6m walk.
3. **Gait Tempo Constraint**: Penalizes mean stride times outside the biological boundary of $[0.7\text{ s}, 2.0\text{ s}]$ (Oberg et al., 1993).
4. **Bilateral Balance Constraint**: Penalizes extreme step imbalances to prevent noise convergence on one side.
5. **TO Event Constraint**: Minimizes the difference between detected HS and TO counts:
   $$\text{Reg}_{\text{TO}} = 0.1 \times \sum_{S \in \{L, R\}} (N_{\text{TO}, S} - N_{\text{HS}, S})^2$$
